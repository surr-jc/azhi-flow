---
title: Approvals
description: Human sign-off as a first-class node, with roles, expiry and structured decisions.
---

An `approval` node pauses the run until a person decides. It is how you gate a risky step and how you satisfy the
[taint rule](/concepts/taint/) for a write that follows untrusted data.

```bash
npx azhi approve <run> <node>                       # approve
npx azhi approve <run> <node> --reject              # reject
npx azhi approve <run> <node> --data '{"drop":"2,4"}'   # with decision data
```

The same approvals are listed in **Mission Control › Approvals**, with the message, payload, who may decide and
the expiry.

## What you can configure

| Setting | Example |
|---|---|
| Role who may decide | `author` |
| Expiry, and what expiry means | The issue splitter expires in 72 hours **as a rejection** |
| A decision schema | Structured answers, for example slice numbers to leave out, or open design questions as selections |
| A message and payload | The diff, findings and an acceptance-criteria checklist |

## Approvals in the shipped workflows

| Workflow | What a person decides |
|---|---|
| AI-SDLC v2 | The **design** (answering open questions) and the **release** (diff, findings, criteria checklist) before any push |
| Issue splitter | The split plan, optionally leaving slices out. **Nothing is written before this** |
| Issue investigation | The exact text of the issue comment |

PR review is deliberately different: it posts one guarded, ledgered, sticky comment without an approval, and its
guard only allows the PR under review.

## Why not just per-tool confirmations?

Per-tool permission prompts belong to an interactive session someone is watching. An approval node is part of a
durable run: it survives restarts, shows in an inbox, has an owner role and an expiry, and is recorded in the
audit log.
